# Assurance and limitations

The universal mixing theorem is a written mathematical proof, not a computer-certified statement. Its load-bearing external inputs are Feng–Guo–Yang's heterogeneous influence/computation-tree results and Miao–Yin's scalar-field entropy and gap results.

Internal checking inspected the stated interfaces and delicate proof transitions: boundary-dependent tree influences, covariance normalization, schedule-conditioned fresh uniforms, original-clock scaling and finite-horizon trajectory errors. Primary reading was bounded to relevant statements, definitions and proof interfaces; the full external papers were not re-proved.

The supplied review was treated as evidence requiring response, not as independent confirmation. No external human peer review, independent reproduction, formal proof-assistant verification or exhaustive novelty certification is claimed. Same-model checks do not create external independence.

The original verifier uses rational arithmetic for finite Gibbs/logistic identities, mixed pinning and tilts, retained-clock kernels, detailed balance and Dirichlet identities. It also checks bounded star examples and isolated-spin indicator energies. Its symbolic entropy expression is not a rational-arithmetic certification of logarithms or an asymptotic LSI divergence. Normal/optimized replay and semantic controls concern only this finite scope; actual execution results belong in the replay receipt.

Neither hashes nor successful CI prove mathematics. Media, DOI registration and archive availability add no scientific assurance.
